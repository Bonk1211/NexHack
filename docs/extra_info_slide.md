## Origin and reviewer feedback this version addresses

This deck evolved from the NexHack 2026 hackathon deck (`docs/slide_guide.md`), after an accelerator partner review (2026-07). Their feedback, and where it landed:

1. **Market validation & traction** — Slide 13 (Validation & Path to Proof): honest pre-traction framing plus a concrete, falsifiable validation-study plan, not fabricated numbers. Slide 14 adds TAM/SAM/SOM sizing for Malaysia + SEA (scope chosen by founder).
2. **Deepen the business model** — Slide 15: unit economics re-derived from the real per-run cost, benchmarked CAC/LTV against published SaaS norms, churn-mitigation argument via the CI-gate mechanic.
3. **Strengthen the moat** — Slide 11 (data moat, real-panel UAT moat, regulatory-timing moat) and Slide 12 (category-validation via funded comparables).
4. **Team ("Why Us") slide** — intentionally omitted. Founder direction: this is a hackathon-originated team, and the credibility argument should be the working, deployed product (Slides 7–10), not bios. If a team slide is wanted later, this is the one deliberate gap left in the deck.
5. **Narrative flow** — restructured to Problem → Why Now → Status Quo → Gap → Solution → Demo/Architecture/Merge-gate/Trust → Moat → Category Validation → Traction/Validation → Market → Business → Roadmap → Ask, per the reviewer's suggested order.
6. **The Ask** — not a dollar figure. Founder direction: this deck targets admission into Xenber's post-hackathon accelerator/mentorship track (`docs/hackathon_deck_instruction.md` §1: "strongest projects may be considered for post-hackathon development, mentorship, pilot opportunities, or funding support"), not a priced VC round. The close asks for program resources, not capital.

## Corrections made during this pass (worth knowing why, not just what)

- **OKU population figure**: the hackathon deck and `docs/business_analyst.md` both cite "608,523 registered OKU (DOSM 2023)." This number could not be verified against any real DOSM release. The correct, current figure is **805,509 (2.4% of population), DOSM 2024**, released 11 Dec 2025 — see reference 1 below. `docs/slide_guide.md` and `docs/business_analyst.md` still have the old number and should be corrected at the source so future decks pull the right figure by default.
- **axe-core "57% of WCAG issues" claim**: real Deque 2021 study measured 57% of accessibility issues **by volume** across 2,000+ audits and 300,000+ issues — not 57% of WCAG success criteria. Prior industry consensus put automated coverage of WCAG success criteria closer to 20–30%. The corrected framing is now in Slide 10.
- **Per-run compute cost**: founder-corrected from the hackathon deck's RM0.04 estimate to the real **~RM0.8/run**. This changed the pricing table (Slide 15): Starter's included quota was cut from 500 to 100 runs/mo, Growth's "unlimited runs" was replaced with a 400-run/mo quota plus metered overage (RM2.00/run Starter, RM1.50/run Growth) — both sized to hold a consistent ~60% gross margin at full utilization instead of the negative margin the old 500-run Starter quota would have produced at the real cost. `docs/slide_guide.md` still says "RM0.04" / "cents per run" in several places and should be corrected at the source.
- **Technical architecture diagram**: the hackathon deck's diagram showed the scripted-mode persona subgraph (`observe → comprehend → decide → act`). That mode is legacy/unused — the actively-used graph is the autonomous cyclic loop (`load → observe → agent → route_next`). Slide 8 now shows the correct, currently-live architecture. This also meant the roadmap (Slide 16) needed a fix: the old roadmap listed "autonomous flow (no script needed)" as a Q3 2026 future item, but it's already built and live today — that item moved to the "NOW (built)" column, which is a genuinely stronger current-state story than the stale roadmap implied.

## Slide 10 addition — persona hyperparameter citation table

The founder's own draft table for this addition contained real citation errors — worth logging exactly what was wrong, since the whole point of the slide is precision and a wrong citation here would be an easy, embarrassing catch for a technical reviewer.

**Corrections made to the founder's draft:**
- **Tap target size**: draft cited "WCAG 2.5.8" for the 44px figure. WCAG 2.5.8 ("Target Size Minimum," AA, WCAG 2.2) actually specifies **24×24 CSS px**, not 44px. The correct citation for 44px is **WCAG 2.5.5** ("Target Size Enhanced," AAA, WCAG 2.1). Corrected in the deck, and framed as a deliberate strength: the codebase's 44px default exceeds the current AA-level legal floor (24px) and targets the stricter AAA/platform-guideline bar instead — a better story than the original mismatched citation would have told anyway.
- **Reading grade**: draft cited "gov.uk plain-language (grade 9)." GOV.UK's actual guidance says "maximum reading age of 9" — UK reading age and US grade level are different scales; reading age 9 is roughly US grade 3–4, not grade 9. Also checked whether the US Plain Writing Act of 2010 mandates a specific numeric grade level — it does not; it only requires "clear, concise, well-organized" writing, no number attached. Replaced with Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (the actual metric computed in the codebase, confirmed against `docs/business_analyst.md`'s own reference to it) plus Doak, Doak & Root's 1996 clinical health-literacy textbook (real, peer-reviewed, cited by AMA's own policy report) as the threshold justification. Also checked and ruled out: the popular "AMA recommends 6th-grade reading level" claim — read AMA's actual 1999 JAMA policy report in full; it contains no numeric grade-level recommendation at all. That claim is citation drift in secondary sources, not traceable to AMA itself. Did not use it.
- **Reading speed**: draft attributed 200 WPM to "NN/g reading-speed studies." NN/g has no dedicated reading-speed study — the 200–250 WPM figure is Jakob Nielsen's own working assumption from a single article, "How Little Do Users Read?", not a research finding. Added Brysbaert (2019), a real peer-reviewed meta-analysis (n=18,573) with a measured 238 WPM average, as the rigorous anchor, and kept the Nielsen estimate as a secondary, correctly-labeled practitioner citation (not mislabeled as "NN/g research").
- **Give-up threshold**: draft cited "Baymard Institute form-abandonment data" for a seconds-based threshold. Baymard's actual published data is rate-based (~70% cart abandonment, 26% cite checkout complexity) — they do not publish a seconds-based confusion-to-abandonment number. Also checked the commonly-repeated claim "NN/g: users abandon after 7–10 seconds of confusion" — this is a misattribution. The real NN/g figure (10 seconds, "Response Time Limits") measures attention span during *system response delay*, a different construct from a persona's patience during an *ambiguous UI flow*. Rather than force either citation to fit, the deck states plainly that `giveup_threshold_s` is a calibrated design parameter without a matching direct citation, and ties that honesty back into the quarterly UAT panel (Slide 11) and validation study (Slide 13) as the mechanism that will eventually ground it empirically. This is a deliberate choice: a table with one honestly-named gap is more credible than a table where every cell has a citation forced to fit.
- **Disability categories**: confirmed both cited classification systems are real — WHO's ICF (2001, 191 member states) and Malaysia JKM's 7 official OKU categories (Pendengaran, Penglihatan, Pertuturan, Fizikal, Pembelajaran, Mental, Pelbagai). Worth double-checking the exact JKM category list against jkm.gov.my directly before this goes in front of anyone who reads Malay natively — the research agent flagged this list as worth a final source-page confirmation, since some older secondary sources use slightly different category names (e.g. "Kurang Upaya Jelas" instead of "Pelbagai").
